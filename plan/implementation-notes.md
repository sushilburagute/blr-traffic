# Implementation notes

The application runs entirely in a browser worker and has landing, simulation, report, guide, and compressed
share routes. Real OSM carriageways and their raw extract are stored in `src/data/corridor`; public copies
are generated by the same deterministic data builder. Flyover/underpass deck geometry is an assumed final
700 m of its associated main segment, with a safe merge at the split point.

## Contract extensions

`src/sim/types.ts` is the implemented contract. Original required fields are preserved. Added fields:

- `ScenarioConfig.liveEvents?`: fixed-tick rain changes and incident additions, so live edits replay exactly.
- `Snapshot.ids`, `segment`, `lane`, `effectiveLanes`: stable vehicle identities, accurate tooltips and exact
  virtual-lane rendering. These typed arrays participate in the same buffer recycling protocol.
- `Segment.bypassFor?` and `osmWayIds?`: main/bypass association and geometry provenance.
- `ToWorker.skipToEnd.untilS?`: report an early endpoint without changing the original demand curve.
- `SimStats.config` and `finished`: report provenance and distinction between completed and early runs.

## Implementation choices and limits

- Node 22 is pinned rather than the plan's Node 20. Yarn Classic is used, with a committed lockfile.
- The default demand is 13,000 veh/h with a 50 km/h speed limit, and terminal signal cycles are 180/150 s.
  Demand and speed are calibrated to inferred trip times (see Calibration below); signal plans are assumptions.
- A named U-turn adds merge friction and a sink/source return trip for an assumed 5% of eligible exits.
  A vehicle can make at most one such return trip; turning arcs are not rendered separately.
- Rain affects speed, headway, acceleration, demand mix and waterlogging. Potholes use reduced wet speed
  caps and a seeded 50% per-encounter late-visibility probability.
- Encroachment and closures reduce accessible lanes; vehicles heading toward a closed next segment exit at
  the previous junction. Fine-grained service-road route choice is simplified.
- Expert sliders cover core settings. Less common arrays and matrices use validated JSON editors.
- UI controls use small Radix/shadcn-style primitives and semantic native controls rather than generating
  unused copies of the full shadcn component catalogue.
- MapLibre/deck.gl use a non-interleaved overlay. Vehicles are interpolated by uid between snapshots
  (`src/components/map/vehicleFrame.ts`). From zoom 16 they are true-scale top-down sprites: a tinted
  body atlas plus a detail atlas with brake lights and blinking indicators, generated by
  `yarn sprites:build` from `TYPE_PARAMS` footprints, and rings mark red-light runs, squeeze-lane use
  and filtering. The engine's rendering-only lateral state (`latM`/`latV`) makes lane changes gradual;
  lateral speed is capped by forward speed so stationary vehicles only creep sideways. Overview is
  top-down and rotates the corridor horizontally.
- The loading screen tracks network, style, atlas, worker warm-up, renderer import, and junction tile
  prefetch at zooms 13–15. Missing basemap tiles fall back gracefully and can load on demand.
- Non-hashed data URLs use bounded cache lifetimes instead of year-long immutable caching.
- Queue thresholds are labelled model categories because an authoritative BTP citation was not verified.
- Annual delay and congestion use completed end-to-end trips; reports explain completion bias and missing
  samples. TomTom is contextual citywide data, used only as supporting evidence for the bands below.
- Vercel deployment, custom-domain DNS, GA DebugView, and external social-card validation need account
  access and have not been verified in this implementation session.

## Calibration (2026-09)

The Monday 9 AM defaults were calibrated so that **uncensored** trip times (`yarn trips`: every vehicle
generated in the first 45 minutes, followed until it arrives, entry wait included) fall inside bands inferred
from public sources. No end-to-end measurement of this corridor was available, so the targets are inferences:

| Monday ~9 AM                        | median    | p90       |
| ----------------------------------- | --------- | --------- |
| Silk Board → Marathahalli           | 30–38 min | 55–65 min |
| Marathahalli → Silk Board           | 35–45 min | 65–80 min |
| Free flow (`engine.stats.freeFlow`) | 13–15 min | —         |

Evidence: TomTom Traffic Index 2025 (Bengaluru morning rush 41 min per 10 km, 14.6 km/h, +94% congestion);
BMTC buses Silk Board–KR Puram (17 km in 50–70 min at peak, 14.6–20 km/h); MoveInSync (tech-park commuters
~18 km/h door to door); taxi free-flow of ~13 min for the 11 km; Bengaluru Traffic Police (BTP) counts of
1.5–2 lakh vehicles/day on Marathahalli–Silk Board and 20–45% year-on-year growth of tech-park private
vehicles in 2025; BTP's assessment that fixing merge/service-road weaving is worth about 20% of capacity.

Levers changed (the minimal set that reaches the bands):

1. **Speed limit 60 → 50 km/h** (`infra.speedLimitKmh` default). It acts as the drivers' free speed, not the
   legal limit: a ~13 min free-flow taxi trip over 11 km is 45–50 km/h. Free flow becomes 13.2 min (was 11.0).
2. **Flyover landings are zipper merges** (`MERGE_PRIORITY` in `src/sim/engine.ts`: the flyover and the
   at-grade road now have equal priority; side-road ramps still give way to both). Previously the at-grade
   road always gave way to traffic coming off the flyover, so eastbound through traffic met no friction at
   Agara, Iblur, Bellandur, Kadubeesanahalli or Marathahalli. On the ORR the platoon released by the at-grade
   signal forces its way in beside the landing traffic (the landing-merge bottleneck BTP describes);
   zip-merging reproduces the resulting capacity loss. At 12,000 veh/h and 50 km/h this alone took
   Silk Board → Marathahalli from 18.7 to 29.9 min median, while Marathahalli → Silk Board, which is limited
   by the Silk Board signal, was unchanged (44.0 → 42.4).
3. **Demand 12,000 → 13,000 veh/h** (`demand.vehPerHour` default). 1.5–2 lakh vehicles/day at an 8–9% peak-hour
   share is ~12,000–18,000 veh/h both ways, and the 2025 growth puts 12,000 at the low end. 13,000 is the
   smallest round increase that brings the eastbound median into band; 14,000 pushes westbound p90 past 80 min.

Kept unchanged: vehicle mix (the ORR carries more cars and cabs than the >70% two-wheeler citywide mix),
`throughShare` 0.65 (0.55–0.60 lengthened side-road entry waits to 3–6 min at p90 without improving the fit),
and signal plans. Tried and not adopted: through traffic allowed onto a flyover only from the median-side
lanes, with lane-positioning pressure for flyover and kerb-side exits (a real weave, but it moved medians by
under 1 min), and letting local trips use flyovers.

Result (seed 42, `yarn trips`): Silk Board → Marathahalli median 31.7 min / p90 56.2; Marathahalli → Silk Board
median 42.7 / p90 79.0 (was 18.5 / 39.2 and 41.3 / 74.6); free flow 13.2 min; side-road entry wait median
0.1 min, p90 0.9–1.0 min; no window arrival left unserved. Seeds 1 and 7 give 31.5–34.9 / 57.4–58.6 eastbound
and 43.5–44.2 / 77.7–81.3 westbound, so westbound p90 sits at the top of its band. Peak queues over the
2-hour run: eastbound Iblur 823 m (reported 400–1000 m at 10 AM), Agara 965 m, Bellandur 522 m,
Kadubeesanahalli 183 m, Marathahalli 147 m; westbound Silk Board 374 m, Agara 459 m, Iblur 416 m,
Bellandur 788 m, Kadubeesanahalli 899 m. Sunday morning stays at free flow (13.3 / 14.6 min medians).
`yarn trips --set path=value` overrides single config fields for further calibration runs.

Share links store differences from the defaults, so changing defaults would silently change old links (and
replayed result links). The scenario format is therefore version 2: `encode` always writes `version`, and
`decode` reads an unversioned (v1) link against the v1 defaults (12,000 veh/h, 60 km/h) via `defaultsFor`.
Full configs (saved state, JSON downloads) carry explicit values and are unaffected; version 1 still parses.
Old links do run on the recalibrated engine (zipper merges), so their results differ from when they were made.

Performance results and outstanding performance targets are recorded in `notes-perf.md`.
